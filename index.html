THE WHY, WITHOUT A LECTURE

Start the lean. Then follow the response.

A brief steer away from the desired turn changes the bike's motion and helps create the lean into the turn.

Tire forces, steering geometry, mass distribution, and wheel rotation contribute. Wheel rotation alone is not the complete explanation.

Grip pressure is an input. Wheel angle and lean are responses. Releasing the grip removes input, not the motion that already exists.

Show the model, assumptions, and actual numbers

A small-angle teaching model

This prototype integrates the coupled lean and steer equations from J. Fajans (2000). The source's damping remains explicit. No balance controller assigns a successful lean.

The example uses inferred Sportster parameters, not measured Royal Enfield handling. The road path uses an additional small-angle, non-slip approximation.

The Classic 350-inspired visuals are an original approximation, not official CAD or vehicle calibration. The virtual grip anchors and steering model remain unchanged.

The mirror surfaces use generated visual reflections, not a live rear-traffic feed. Use the external inset to observe the model's actual lean and path.

Directional forces: steering effects only

The input example uses a straight virtual bar, a 0.30 m grip lever, and an ideal vertical steering axis. Only the steering component of the force moment enters the model.

Side and top-down presses project zero steering torque in this example. That does not mean these presses are harmless or have no effect on a real motorcycle. Body reactions and real bar geometry are not modeled.

Numerical integration

A fixed 240-step-per-second RK4 solver advances the physics. Render interpolation and comfort camera settings do not change the dynamics.

The angular pulse response has a separate matrix-exponential reference. Numerical agreement is not proof of real motorcycle accuracy.

Example parameters

Combined mass: 300 kg. Wheelbase: 1.54 m. Wheel radius: 0.29 m. Steering damping: 3 Nm s.

KNOW WHAT THIS VERSION DOES NOT SHOW

A model boundary is not a fall.

The trial pauses beyond 10 degrees of lean or 5 degrees of steering. Those are conservative design boundaries, not real motorcycle safety limits.

This version does not calculate tire slip, grip loss, ground contact after a fall, or walking-speed rider balance.

Keep experiments inside this simulator. Use qualified hands-on rider instruction for practical riding skills.
