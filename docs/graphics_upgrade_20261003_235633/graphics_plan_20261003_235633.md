**Task start:** October 3, 2026, 23:56 UTC+05:30.
**Plan date:** October 4, 2026.
**Purpose:** Replace the initial wireframe with a richer rider view while preserving the working tutorial.
**Status:** Implemented and verified on October 4, 2026. Learner visual feedback remains pending.

## 1. Target and assumptions

[Inference] Use the traditional Royal Enfield Classic 350 cockpit as the reference.
The earlier request named the Classic/Bullet family.
The user is unavailable to select an exact model year.

Create an original visual approximation, not an official CAD reproduction.
Study official product images and manuals for recognizable features.
Keep the current handling model and its limitations unchanged.
Visual realism does not establish Royal Enfield handling accuracy.

## 2. Highest-value cockpit details

Build the parts that the rider sees first:

- A curved chrome handlebar with visible tube thickness, risers, clamps, and bolt heads.
- Black ribbed grips, bar-end caps, and switch housings.
- Separate clutch and brake levers with pivots and cable or hose routing.
- Two round mirrors on chrome stalks.
- A round analog speedometer with a chrome bezel, original dial artwork, and a small LCD-style area.
- A painted classic-style tank with a chrome fuel cap and a detailed headlamp housing.

Treat buttons and levers as visual details unless their behavior already exists.
Do not add braking, throttle, gears, navigation, or vibration simulation.
Do not invent working indicators for vehicle systems that the model excludes.

## 3. Materials, environment, and camera

Use solid physically based materials instead of wireframe lines.
Give chrome, rubber, painted metal, glass, and cast housings different surface responses.
Use procedural textures and a generated lighting environment.
Use daylight, soft shadows, readable lane markings, roadside grass, and restrained distant scenery.
Avoid photorealism claims or a heavy environment framework.

Compose the rider view around the handlebar and instrument panel.
Keep both grips, mirrors, levers, and the dial visible at desktop and mobile sizes.
Keep the tank partly visible without dominating the whole frame.
Keep the road and horizon readable.
Preserve the rear inset, force arrows, live cues, comfort mode, and active-input feedback.

If mirrors use generated reflections, identify them as visual reflections rather than safety-grade rear-view instrumentation.

## 4. Data and physics boundary

The renderer reads the same calculated lean, steer, heading, and position.
It does not generate a successful turn or modify the dynamics.

Add actual simulation speed and time to the scene contract.
Drive the analog needle from speed, not a decorative animation.
Drive wheel rotation from constant-speed travel and simulation time.
A paused trial keeps its actual model speed while time remains paused.

Keep virtual grip centers at lateral positions of plus/minus 0.30 m.
Keep their fore-aft relationship to the ideal steering axis unchanged.
Shape the chrome tube and surrounding parts around those anchors.
Do not silently substitute measured-looking graphics for a calibrated force geometry.
Document the remaining ideal-bar and vertical-axis assumptions.

## 5. Asset policy

Use official photographs as references only.
Create all geometry, dial artwork, textures, and environment assets procedurally.
Do not bundle OEM photographs, logos, third-party models, or unlicensed HDR images.
Keep all authored files in this project.
Keep generated builds, browser output, and screenshots in `Q:\pp_exp\pp_countersteering_artifacts`.
